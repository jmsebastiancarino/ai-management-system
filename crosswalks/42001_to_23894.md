# Crosswalk: ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS) ↔ ISO/IEC 23894 (AI Risk Management)

ISO/IEC 42001 requires AI risk to be managed but is light on *how*. ISO/IEC 23894 is the companion guidance that supplies the method — applying the general risk-management process of ISO 31000 to the specific characteristics of AI. This crosswalk shows where the AIMS controls draw on 23894's risk guidance.

| Catalog ID | Control | ISO 42001 | ISO 23894 (clause) | What 23894 contributes |
|------------|---------|-----------|--------------------|------------------------|
| AIMS-DATA-1 | Training Data Governance | A.7.x | 6.2 | Risk sources in data: quality, bias, representativeness |
| AIMS-RISK-1 | AI Risk Assessment and Impact Analysis | Clause 6.1, A.5.x | 6.4, 6.5 | Risk identification & analysis tailored to AI harms |
| AIMS-VAL-1 | Model Validation and Performance Testing | A.6.2, A.8.2 | 6.5 | Evaluating risk via performance, robustness, fairness |
| AIMS-TRANS-1 | Transparency and System Documentation | A.8.3, A.8.4 | 6.3 | Communicating AI risk to interested parties |
| AIMS-MON-1 | Operational Monitoring & Drift | A.9.3, A.9.4 | 6.6 | Monitoring & review of AI risk over the lifecycle |

**Why three standards, not one:** 42001 is the certifiable *system* (what you must have), the NIST AI RMF is the *risk vocabulary* (how to reason about it), and 23894 is the *risk method* (how to run the process). Mapping a single control across all three is what makes "we manage AI risk" defensible to an auditor rather than aspirational.

*ISO clause references are summarized, not quoted. Mappings are illustrative and synthetic.*
